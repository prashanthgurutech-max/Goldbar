💍 Jewellery vs Coin

The same 10g of gold costs much more as jewellery than as a coin. Here's exactly why — and how to calculate the fair price.

Compare Costs Side by Side

⚙ Jewellery cost details

Making charges vary widely: ₹200–400/g machine-made, ₹500–2000/g handmade. Wastage (karigar loss) is 3–8% for most designs.

Why Jewellery Costs More

1. Making Charges (Karigar fee)

Charged per gram for the artisan's work. Ranges from ₹200/g for simple machine-made chains to ₹2000/g+ for intricate handmade designs like Kundan or temple jewellery. Always ask for it separately in writing — it's negotiable.

2. Wastage (Tulaabharam)

Gold is "wasted" in filings and polish during crafting. Jewellers charge this as a % of gold weight (typically 3–8%). You're paying for gold that doesn't end up in your jewellery. Always ask them to justify the wastage %. For machine-made items it should be under 3%.

3. GST — 3% on total price

3% GST is charged on the full invoice including making charges. You cannot claim this back unlike other goods. This GST is a pure cost — gold coins from banks also attract 3% GST.

4. Coins: no making, minimal premium

BIS-hallmarked 24K coins from banks or certified dealers have only a 2–5% premium over gold rate. No making charges, no wastage. When you sell, you get close to the day's rate. Jewellery buyers offer 5–15% less than spot.

Smart Buyer Checklist

ℹ️ Prices are indicative. Making charges, wastage, and gold rates vary across jewellers and regions. Always verify BIS hallmark before purchasing. Resale value of jewellery depends on the buyer and design — plain jewellery gets better resale.
